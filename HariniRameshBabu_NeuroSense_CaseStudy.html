NeuroSense: Product Overview
v1.0 · Harini Ramesh Babu · Northeastern MEM · Q2 2026
Product Vision
A clinical AI copilot that detects Parkinson's Disease 4+ years earlier than current standard of care, by surfacing voice biomarker signals directly inside the workflows neurologists and primary care physicians already use every day.
The One-Line Pitch
"Parkinson's whispers in the voice years before it shouts in the hands. NeuroSense listens."
Stage
Active DevelopmentPilot: 2 Boston hospitalsTarget market: Healthtech B2B
Why Now?
Three converging forces: (1) FDA's 2025 framework finally cleared the regulatory path for AI clinical decision support. (2) Epic and athenahealth opened FHIR R4 APIs so integration is now possible without needing custom engineering. (3) Post-COVID, neurologists are overwhelmed. Automated pre-screening frees specialist time for patients who actually need it.
Problem Statement
Why this product needs to exist
The Core Problem
Parkinson's Disease affects 7–10 million people globally. Most are diagnosed at Stage 3–4, when motor symptoms are already severe. The average patient experiences symptoms for 5+ years before diagnosis. This is not a science problem. The biomarkers exist. It is a product and workflow problem. The signals aren't connected to the clinicians who need them.
Why current solutions fail
🧲
MRI scans can't detect early-stage PD. They only show structural damage that appears years in. Cost: $2,000+ per scan. Not scalable for screening.
👩‍⚕️
Clinical exams have a 25% misdiagnosis rate by non-specialists. Most patients see a GP first, who has no screening tool.
📱
Existing ML research (including my undergrad project) showed high accuracy but was never connected to EHR workflows. It lived in Colab notebooks, not clinics.
The PM insight
Voice biomarkers appear 4+ years before visible tremors. A 60-second voice recording, analysed at a routine GP visit, could flag at-risk patients before symptoms are visible. The ML is solved. The product gap is the workflow integration.

Primary buyer
Hospital systems and ACOs (not individual physicians). The buyer is the VP of Clinical Technology or CMO. They pay per health system per year. Physicians are users, not buyers. This distinction shaped every product decision.
Jobs-to-be-Done
What users hire NeuroSense to do
Framework used: JTBD (Christensen)
I'm not building features. I'm solving jobs. Each job came directly from user interviews, not assumptions.
Functional Jobs
🎯
Job 1: "Help me identify which of my at-risk patients should be referred to neurology without adding 20 minutes to my workflow."
→ NeuroSense solution: Automated voice screening embedded in existing EHR check-in flow
📊
Job 2: "Give me something I can show to the patient to explain why I'm referring them and not just a black-box number."
→ NeuroSense solution: Plain-language explanation panel showing which voice features drove the score
🛡️
Job 3: "I need to prove to compliance that our AI tools are audited, governed, and FDA-compliant before I sign any contract."
→ NeuroSense solution: Built-in governance dashboard, HIPAA audit log, FDA SaMD Class II classification
Emotional Job
"Help me feel like I didn't miss something." Every primary care physician in our interviews said a variation of this. The emotional job is about reducing guilt and improving confidence, not just efficiency.
Scope & Out-of-Scope
What we build and the discipline to say no
In Scope for v3
✅
Voice biomarker analysis via 60-second recording during standard check-in
✅
Risk score + plain-language explanation surfaced inside Epic / athenahealth
✅
Human-in-the-loop: physician approves before any EHR update
✅
Governance dashboard: HIPAA logs, bias audit results, FDA compliance status
Out of Scope for v3, with rationale
❌
Patient-facing mobile app. RICE score 440 vs. EHR integration 920. Physicians are the bottleneck, not patients. We solve the bottleneck first.
❌
Autonomous EHR updates. This would cross the FDA's line from CDS to autonomous SaMD, which would trigger a 510(k) requirement and delaying launch by 18 months. Not worth it for v3.
❌
Treatment recommendation. We detect. We do not prescribe. Scope creep here would spook every hospital compliance team we're trying to sign.
The PM principle here
A great PM is defined as much by what they say no to as what they ship. Every out-of-scope decision has a documented rationale. That is what separates product thinking from feature collecting.
Success Metrics
How we know if the product is working
North Star Metric
Detection Lead Time per Patient: the average number of years earlier NeuroSense identifies at-risk patients vs. traditional clinical examination. Currently sitting at 4.2 years. Target: 5.0 years by end of 2026.

Why this metric? Because "model accuracy" is an engineering metric. Accuracy going from 94.9% to 95.5% means nothing to a hospital. A patient being diagnosed 4 years earlier changes their life, and it's measurable in outcomes data.
Product Metrics
📊
Clinician activation rate: % of onboarded physicians who complete their first EHR-approved risk referral within 7 days. Target: 60%
⏱️
Workflow add-on time: Seconds added to standard check-in by the voice recording. Target: <90 seconds
✅
Physician override rate: % of AI risk flags that physicians dismiss. High override = model needs recalibration. Target: <15%
Guardrail Metrics (do not harm)
🚨
False negative rate must stay below 5%. Missing a true PD case is our worst outcome. Non-negotiable.
🚨
Demographic accuracy gap must stay below 3%. Weekly bias audit. Model retraining triggered if gap exceeds threshold.
Product Roadmap

5 versions, all justified

Each version was not planned from day one. It was earned by evidence from user research, regulatory signals, and market data. This is what evidence-based roadmapping looks like.

v1: The Undergrad ML Model
2022 · St. Joseph's College of Engineering
✓ Shipped
Built an XGBoost classifier on the UCI Parkinson's voice dataset. Achieved 94.9% accuracy. Submitted as an academic project. This was an engineering deliverable, not a product. There were no users, no workflow, no business model. But it proved the technical hypothesis: voice biomarkers can predict PD with clinical-grade accuracy.
94.9% accuracy
22 features
0 users
PM lesson from v1: Technical proof-of-concept ≠ product. I had an answer to "can we detect PD from voice?" but not to "would a clinician trust this, use it, or pay for it?" That gap drove everything that came next.
v2: Explainability Layer and FDA Strategy
Q3 2025 · Triggered by user research
✓ Shipped
Conducted 12 physician interviews. The #1 finding: "I can't act on a black-box score." Every neurologist said they would not use a tool that couldn't explain its reasoning to a patient or a colleague. Added SHAP explainability. Registered as FDA SaMD Class II. This unblocked the first hospital pilot.
12 interviews
FDA Class II registered
1 pilot site
Why this, not a mobile app: I was tempted to build a patient-facing app first. User research said no. Physicians are the bottleneck so I fixed the bottleneck. The app could wait.
v3: Agentic AI and EHR Integration
Q2 2026 · Current version
→ Now
The single biggest learning from v2 pilot: "We love it, but we won't use a separate tool. It has to be inside Epic." Built HL7 FHIR R4 integration. Added NeuroAgent for batch processing. Human-in-the-loop governance built to address hospital compliance requirements and the new 2026 shadow AI governance standards.
2 EHR integrations
4.2yr detection lead
247 screened
The governance decision: Adding HITL (human-in-the-loop) slows the product down but it was the right call. Hospital compliance teams won't sign without it, and it reduces FDA regulatory risk. Short term friction for long term adoption.
v4: Wearable and Multi-modal Detection
Q4 2026 · In discovery phase
Next
Wrist accelerometer data from consumer wearables (Apple Watch Series 10, Fitbit) combined with voice = multi-modal detection. Hypothesis: combining tremor pattern data with voice biomarkers could push accuracy above 98% and also enable continuous home monitoring and not just in-clinic screening.
98%+ target accuracy
Continuous monitoring
Consumer hardware
Still in discovery: I need to answer three questions first. One: Does wearable data actually improve sensitivity or just add noise? (2) Who owns the wearable data liability? (3) Will patients consent to continuous monitoring? I am not building until I have answers to all three.
v5: Population Health at Scale
2027 · Concept stage
Future
Integrate with telehealth platforms for population-level screening. Target market: Medicare Advantage plans and ACOs that are incentivised to find high-risk patients early. This is a different business model (per-member-per-month vs. per-health-system) and needs its own discovery process.
Why it's on the roadmap: The market signal is there. Population health platforms are moving toward proactive AI-driven intervention right now. But we won't scope it until v4 proves multi-modal detection works and we have real clinical outcome data to show payers.
User Research

12 interviews. 3 pivots.

I talked to 12 physicians and clinical administrators in Boston. Here is what they said and how it changed the product.

🗂️
Research Methodology
Semi-structured interviews, 30–45 min each. 8 neurologists and primary care physicians at MGH and Brigham & Women's. 4 clinical technology leaders at Boston-area health systems. Synthesized using affinity mapping. All quotes anonymized.
🎯
Research Questions
Primary: What is the biggest friction point in your current PD screening or referral workflow?

Secondary: What would make you trust and actually use an AI tool for clinical decision support?

Assumption to test: Physicians want more sensitive detection tools.
"I don't need a more accurate tool. I need a tool I can use in 90 seconds without switching out of Epic."
— Primary Care Physician, MGH
"If I can't explain why the AI flagged this patient to their face, I won't act on it. Full stop."
— Neurologist, Brigham & Women's
"We've been burned by AI vendors before. Show me your governance framework before you show me your accuracy numbers."
— VP Clinical Technology, Boston Health System
Key Insights and what changed because of them
1
Insight: Workflow friction beats accuracy as a barrier to adoption. 10 of 12 physicians said they would not use a tool that required switching to a new interface. This killed the plan to build a standalone web app first. → Pivot: EHR integration became the #1 v3 priority, moving ahead of the patient-facing app.
2
Insight: Explainability is a clinical requirement, not a feature. "A number without a reason is not a clinical tool." Every neurologist said they couldn't communicate a black-box risk score to a patient or a referring physician. → Pivot: SHAP explainability moved from v3 to v2, shipped before EHR integration.
3
Insight: The buyer (CTO/CMO) cares about governance more than accuracy. Clinical technology leaders asked about HIPAA compliance, audit logs, and FDA classification before asking about the model. → Pivot: Built the full governance framework into v3. Made it a visible product feature, not a backend concern.
4
Surprise finding: Physicians do not trust AI as a concept. They trust process. "I trust this tool because I can see who approved it, when, and why. Just the way they trust a lab result." Human-in-the-loop was not seen as a weakness. It was a trust signal. → Decision: Made the physician sign-off step visible and prominent in the UX, not hidden.

💡
The counter-intuitive cut
The patient mobile app had the highest reach (40,000 patients/month) and was the most requested feature in patient surveys. I still cut it from v3.

Reason: Patients can't drive adoption. They can't install our tool in their GP's Epic workflow. The physician is the bottleneck and the app doesn't solve that. High reach on the wrong user = low actual impact.
⚖️
RICE limitations I acknowledge
RICE is a conversation starter, not a verdict. I adjusted raw scores for: strategic alignment (governance unlocks the contract), regulatory risk (anything touching prescribing), and reversibility (easy to add later vs. hard to unwind).

A prioritization framework without judgment is just a spreadsheet. The PM's job is the judgment call.
